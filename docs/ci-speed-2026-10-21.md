## Where time goes

[Queue run 39521142237](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/actions/runs/39521142237)
took 696 seconds through CI Gate: 36 seconds initial orchestration, 215 seconds
overlapping Fast Gate/build, 3 seconds transition, 422 seconds concurrent OFF/ON
test jobs, and 20 seconds final aggregation. Tier 2 compilation is already
overlapped. The build step's 96 seconds includes orchestration, not just compiler
CPU time. Across eight inspected successful runs, that step took 66–96 seconds.

The ON suite registered 341 tests and took 331.24 seconds. Five RUN_SERIAL tests
had durations of 82.79 seconds (deinterlacing), 59.26 (async audio delay), 38.87
(PipeWire resume), 34.61 (VLC pacing), and 23.60 (audio clock drift). Increasing
CTest parallelism cannot overlap a RUN_SERIAL test with other tests in its
environment. Three PulseAudio device scenarios skipped; preserve that distinction
between registration and executed coverage.

## Decisions and current evidence

1. **Overlap Qt with Fast Gate — implement and measure.**
   [PR #1582](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/pull/1582) starts Qt after
   successful scope detection while keeping both jobs required. Configure,
   build, and test phases get separate successful-phase wall times. In its
   [PR run](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/actions/runs/39523981726),
   Qt ran 00:53:16–00:56:23 UTC and Fast Gate ran 00:53:29–00:55:42: all
   133 seconds of Fast Gate overlapped Qt. This verifies scheduling, not a
   causal before/after speedup. Failed-gate wasted Qt time remains to measure.
2. **Two isolated Tier 2 shards per mode — retain rollout for day measurement.**
   [PR #1583](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/pull/1583) reuses the build
   artifact and duration planner. Four runners replace two; each keeps
   RUN_SERIAL intact and verifies all plans partition its actual CTest registry
   exactly once. Missing plans fail closed. Both its PR and queue passed with
   341 registered tests per mode and the same three PulseAudio device skips.
   The PR split OFF 169+172 and ON 171+170; all 338 executed tests per mode
   passed. No C test or RUN_SERIAL property was changed. The first queue's
   longest test step took 178 seconds; required gate took 485 seconds. The
   increased runner cost remains part of the final acceptance decision.
3. **Prepared Tier 2 environment — defer deployment under #1592.**
   Cached apt archives still leave 30–52 seconds installation per job. A
   prepared image must beat stock-image setup including pull/restore time,
   with a pinned reproducible package set. Existing
   [#525](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/issues/525) gates dependency
   producers on measured payoff and explicit sign-off for repository/workflow
   creation. The [local experiment](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/blob/c2af3f9c3e7945566b88440dffb14c255aeebd96/docs/ci-evidence/2026-10-21-tier2-environment-experiment.json)
   measured 150.634 seconds for a fresh stock container with seeded apt archives
   versus 4.343 seconds for a fresh container from a resident prepared image.
   Both package/version fingerprints matched. The prepared image is 541 MB
   uncompressed. These local figures exclude registry publication and a fresh
   runner's download/unpack; local installation is much slower than CI. The
   current credential cannot inventory registry packages (`read:packages`,
   HTTP 403), and no compatible published immutable image was verified.
   [#1592](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/issues/1592) records the
   publication/access and fresh-runner benchmark gate. Keep stock Ubuntu and
   apt caching in this batch; no producer, workflow or image publication was
   introduced, and no net CI environment gain is claimed.
4. **Rust compilation — retain settings; measure individual rebuilds next.**
   The three inspected workspace test steps combine about 35–41 seconds of
   Cargo-reported build/link work with 59–62 seconds between first test launch
   and last test-result output. The compiler is a substantial portion, but
   treating the whole 96–102 second step as compilation overstates it. The
   [phase evidence](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/blob/c2af3f9c3e7945566b88440dffb14c255aeebd96/docs/ci-evidence/2026-10-21-rust-test-phase-profile.json) retains
   run IDs, command timestamps and output spans. Merged
   [#1590](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/pull/1590) extracts these
   with `--include-logs`, including Soldr timestamp prefixes and raw or escaped
   ANSI colors, without another build. Missing markers remain unknown.
   Linker-only cost, exact process teardown time and Cargo rebuild reasons
   remain unavailable from these log lines. Controlled per-unit profiling and
   rebuild/fingerprint work remain with
   [#1286](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/issues/1286). Keep the Rust
   wrapper bypass and fingerprint safeguards; no compiler-setting speedup is
   claimed from three unmatched revisions with restored cook archives.
5. **Scheduling gaps — measure intervals and remove empty publication work.**
   In [run 39520254679](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/actions/runs/39520254679),
   Tier 2 aggregation ended at 00:26:12 UTC, CI Gate was created at 00:26:13,
   and a runner started it at 00:28:29. Thus 136 of the 137 seconds were after
   job creation, rather than compilation or a running dependency. The API
   does not establish the dispatch delay's cause. The baseline also contains
   job creation-to-start intervals of 427 seconds for a Tier 2 build and 818 seconds for a Swift
   format job. Report job-created-to-started duration separately from job
   execution. Creation-to-start intervals can include dependency waiting and
   cannot isolate dispatch latency without inspecting readiness; avoid inferring
   capacity exhaustion or changing concurrency
   policy from one observation. In the shard PR, the Tier 2 publication job
   waited 96 seconds, ran for four seconds and skipped every work step. Merged
   [#1589](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/pull/1589) checks for an
   eligible proof or master timing publication before requesting that runner.
   Matrix failures remain failures of the required gate. Interval and sample
   tail reporting landed in [#1585](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/pull/1585).

### Revision changes and final decisions

This was a representative day of actual repository work, with changing source
and policy. It was not a day on a frozen implementation. In particular:

- **Qt overlap: retain.** 19 of 20 PR and 14 of 15 queue successful Qt/Fast Gate
  job pairs overlapped; median overlap was 184.5 and 144 seconds. These counts
  include all source cohorts. No observed failed Fast Gate run had an active
  Qt job, so failed-gate wasted Qt cost is unmeasured, not proven zero for
  future runs. Keep both final dependencies and cancellation conditions.
- **Sharding: superseded.** The mixed-layout day cohorts showed lower Linux gate medians
  with more runner time. [#1643](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/pull/1643)
  merged at 20:43:20 UTC, removing nine serial barriers after resource isolation
  and a core ordering fix. It retained deinterlace RUN_SERIAL. At the user's
  request, [#1663](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/pull/1663) merged at
  22:22:41 UTC, restoring one complete suite per backend and halving repeated
  test-job setup/download work. Keep that current unsharded policy. Do not
  restore four runners based on the earlier cohort result. Optional local
  sharding tools remain available.
- **Prepared environment: defer under #1592.** The original local experiment
  excludes a fresh runner's image transfer. No net CI saving is established;
  keep stock Ubuntu plus apt caching until its publication and transfer
  benchmark gate is satisfied.
- **Rust compile settings: retain; further profiling under #1286.** Sampled
  Fast Gate workspace commands reported 37.39–43.79 seconds build/link and
  59.72–64.29 seconds observed test output. A changed-crate queue command was
  only 2.92 seconds build/link and 2.88 seconds output: do not pool these
  different scopes. Logs still cannot isolate linker-only time or rebuild
  reasons. No extra compilation or wrapper/fingerprint changes were made.
- **Empty publisher skip and timing metrics: retain.** Required failures stay
  failures. Master timing publication was verified after the original rollout;
  remote cache publication remains master-only. Several independent cache
  changes (#1670, #1671, #1672, #1675, #1680 and #1681) also landed late in the
  day. They and the hybrid-default change #1588 prevent attributing the entire
  day to #1581. Qt pruning #1690 landed after the observation window.

### Measurement limits and follow-through

The day supports adopting Qt overlap, retaining the publication guard and phase
instrumentation, and recording the cost of the temporary shard experiment.
It does not establish a universal speedup. Cache hits in sampled build logs
indicate warm restores, while some compilations remain cold; missing cache
markers are unknown. Setup, restore/save, build/configure and test step times
are retained separately in lane summaries. Qt phase markers and Cargo phase
markers are in sampled logs; build-step wall time is not compiler CPU time.
Creation-to-start includes dependency waits and cannot identify runner
contention by itself. The earlier 136-second post-creation gate delay remains
unexplained; further readiness/dispatch diagnosis stays with the overall queue
runtime tracker [#930](https://github.com/obs-rust/obs-studio/issues/930).
This batch adds measurement rather than changing runner policy.

Further prepared-image work is gated by #1592 and rebuild/link/cache work by
#1286. Preserve the current complete OFF/ON suites, required checks,
differential tiers and cache safeguards while pursuing those follow-ups.
